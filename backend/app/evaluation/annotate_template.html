How to label (click to collapse) — judge only from the text shown; do not use what you know about the papers.
1. Stance — look only at what each claim concludes. Ignore differences in setting for this step.
Agree: same conclusion about the same relationship (same kind of method/factor, same kind of outcome).
Oppose: conflicting conclusions about the same relationship (opposite effect, or one reports an effect/advantage the other denies).
Partial: same relationship, but neither a clear conflict nor clear agreement (one narrows, weakens or conditions the other).
Unrelated: not the same relationship. Sharing a topic or a method name is not enough.
2. Context — only for Agree / Oppose / Partial. Use the claims and passages.
Comparable: the text states conditions (dataset, scale, training regime, task, population, metric…) and they are essentially the same.
Different: the text states a difference in conditions that could plausibly explain why the findings differ (or, for Agree, shows the finding holds in a new setting).
Not stated: the text does not say enough about the conditions of at least one claim to tell.
Tie-breakers
Only one claim states its conditions → Not stated (not Different).
Two explicitly general claims ("there is an inherent trade-off" vs "the trade-off is not inherent") → Comparable.
"Our fix closes/reduces the gap" vs "the gap exists" → Partial (the first concedes the gap).
Two different named methods ("A is state of the art" vs "B is state of the art") → Unrelated.
Flag a claim as "not a real claim" if it is garbled, a heading fragment, a question, or only describes what was done. Still label the pair if you can.
